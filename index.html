PLAY FIRST / LEARN AS YOU GO

Easy to start.
Room to master.

Your first minute

WASD + mouse move and look. Space jumps. LMB / RMB use your assigned left/right ability pair; follow the preparation and release cue. Tab moves to the next pair. Z dodges, X counters and E interacts.

1–8 select a skill and its pair; Q casts that selected skill. 0 cycles three editable pages covering all 24 techniques. Each page has four pairs: 1/2, 3/4, 5/6 and 7/8. Your initial combat pair is Energy/Matter; Flight stays in slot 1 of the default first page.

Make the controls yours

F1 → Controls → Edit computer keys & 24-skill quickbar. Search, rebind a single key or mouse button, assign techniques, then Apply. Cancel leaves the current controls untouched.

Conflicts are rejected. F1 stays as menu recovery. Optional quick-cast starts OFF; ultimates remain separate. The current bar uses keys, not drag-and-drop.

THE GAME / CHARGE HAS CONSEQUENCES

Power you can measure.
Reactions you can test.

One charge, one output budget

Ultimate charge now scales damage, local radius and environmental strength together. Cover is checked before walls collapse; radial falloff reduces edge damage. Changing the loadout or effective level cancels the charge instead of exchanging it for a stronger move.

Relic finishers scale with level instead of dealing an unconditional 10,000 damage. Armed planetary strikes require level 2,000, full charge and a destructive family; support skills do not secretly destroy worlds.

Bounded debris and water

Nearby rubble receives size-sensitive, capped impulses. Wet basins emit short-lived, shore-tapered ripples without creating extra water; submerged dense debris slows and sinks.

Local effects stay bounded for Fidelity. These are gameplay approximations—not measured blast physics, a fluid solver, or full planetary fracture. The existing population caps and species are preserved rather than inflated beyond the laptop budget.

The tier names describe progression and permissions. Local blasts stay under 30 metres. A planetary strike changes saved surface state; Universal and System do not mean physically simulating the destruction of every distant object. The protected level-7,000 world reset remains separate.

05 / KNOW WHAT YOU ARE GETTING

Built to grow. Clearly labelled.

What changes in R10 Fidelity Visual?

Painted World Pass 9 adds an offline brain to source preparation through START_HERE.cmd or Start Prep, including the separate HighEnd startup. It records the current project, engine, preparation stages and first relevant error, with before-and-after configuration evidence. It uses built-in rules and needs no downloaded AI model or service. After successful editor preparation, a change limited to Config/DefaultEngine.ini can trigger one real rebuild against the final configuration. Other or repeated changes stop with a report; the final readiness checks remain strict.

If source preparation stops after its report starts, the window prints the local path: EclipseGame/Saved/Diagnostics/PrepBrain/Latest.html. Open the report for that run, then use Tools/Advanced/COLLECT_ASSET_DIAGNOSTICS.cmd in the same extraction to collect the evidence. If startup stops before printing a report path, keep the visible error and run the collector. The report helps explain preparation; it does not automatically change graphics settings or prove rendered quality. Extract the complete repair into a new folder and keep your earlier compiled folder intact.

R10 Fidelity Visual improves close scenery through procedural trees with irregular crowns and native houses with pitched roofs, gables, trim and chimneys. It adds a seed-stable orbital star backdrop, dim cosmic haze, and radius-scaled impact rings without adding simulated worlds or extra damage. It retains the R6 physically lit materials, three explicit rendering budgets and local performance capture. Balanced is the starting choice for i5-1135G7 / Iris Xe. Low reduces the rendering load; Enhanced spends more on image detail and nearby shadows. These are settings, not measured laptop frame-rate promises.

The compact typed conversation panel has optional game-interaction buttons. Built-in replies use native work, mood, weather and memory instead of a generic help-menu. A real generative model remains optional and separately configured. The established paired abilities, pictured inventory, local Atlas, 90-minute clock, living-world recovery, companions, progression and bounded physics remain. Both Fidelity and HighEnd source profiles are in this complete ZIP; a standalone Windows player requires a successful Windows build and playtest.

What do the pictured inventory and local Atlas show?

Illustrated inventory cards show your actual relics, suit, field weapons and armor, cargo, meals and companions. Quantities, ownership and equipped state come from the existing game systems.

The detailed local Atlas draws sampled terrain, existing paths, districts, places and objectives, with a scale, legend and your heading. It helps connect the map to the nearby world; the destination catalogue remains at 389. Companion markers follow the trust and nearby-encounter rules described above.

What has—and has not—been tested?

Portable native gameplay and geometry tests, local updater/packaging tests and browser-interface tests accompany the source. Synthetic build-output fixtures are labelled as such. They do not establish an Unreal build, shader compilation, rendered quality or frame rate on your laptop.

Historical A5.5 verification includes actual portable native controls/clock/weather/save tests, Python/filesystem and explicit Unreal adapters, browser interaction with mocked release responses, and final archive byte delivery. Older tests are retained as regression coverage. These are not Unreal screenshots, real model inference, physical haptics or laptop FPS measurements.

Which Fidelity budget should I use?

Start with Balanced on Iris Xe. In the game, use F1 → Settings → Detail: Balanced to cycle Low, Balanced and Enhanced. At a 1920 × 1080 game window, Low uses 50% internal resolution (about 960 × 540), Balanced 66.667% (about 1280 × 720), and Enhanced 83.333% (about 1600 × 900). All three keep DX11/SM5, temporal anti-aliasing upsampling and a 30 FPS cap. A cap is not a guaranteed minimum.

Low disables nearby shadows. Balanced budgets one 512-pixel shadow cascade over 30 m; Enhanced uses one 1024-pixel cascade over 45 m. Your rendering choice does not expand the simulation or change your world. A newer laptop year does not automatically select HighEnd.

After preparation, Tools/Advanced/CAPTURE_FIDELITY_PERFORMANCE.cmd opens the Fidelity game and records a local sample after five seconds of warmup and thirty uninterrupted seconds of play. The report labels the RHI and primary GPU and measures runtime Tick intervals, including frame pacing and the cap. It does not measure GPU execution time or upload anything. Pause, travel or graphics changes restart the sample.

Controller and graphics compatibility

The Fidelity profile keeps DX11/SM5 and TAA upsampling, with Low, Balanced and Enhanced rendering budgets. Balanced is the default for the reported Iris Xe hardware. The cap is not a guaranteed minimum. The bounded performance fallback can reduce scene resolution and nearby shadows during active play. Switching to another app does not trigger a quality reduction. It never changes worlds, save files or gameplay rules.

Use a USB data cable for a first controller test. Standard gamepad bindings, live axis/button display and reconnect safety are included. PlayStation controllers use a compatible input layer such as Steam Input; PS Remote Play is console streaming, not a Windows gamepad driver. The package includes offline controller help. Physical Bluetooth, adaptive triggers and advanced haptics are not certified.
